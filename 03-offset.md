# Lesson 3 — The offset

Time: 75 minutes. Group: one board example, one demo bend, then two student bends. The 45° piece can be board-only if the rack is getting thin.

Students leave able to space two marks, add shrink in the right direction, and bend both marks at the same angle in one plane.

## Before they come in

Tape the bench card on the demo bench. Draw a wall line on the board: a box at the left, a 6 in. obstruction whose near edge is 60 in. from the box, pipe running into the page from the left. You will point at that drawing for the whole lesson.

## Open

An offset is two equal bends in opposite directions. The pipe leaves on a new line, parallel to the line it had. Use it to step off a wall, clear a beam, or line up with a knockout.

Two bends, so two marks. The angle sets both numbers you need. This class defaults to 30° because the spacing is twice the depth and the shrink is a quarter inch per inch of depth. You can do that math with a tape and no phone.

Write the chart. Read 30° twice.

| Angle | Multiplier | Shrink per inch of depth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10° | 6 | 1/16 in. |
| 22.5° | 2.6 | 3/16 in. |
| 30° | 2 | 1/4 in. |
| 45° | 1.4 | 3/8 in. |
| 60° | 1.2 | 1/2 in. |

The multiplier is how far apart the marks are, in inches of pipe per inch of rise. The shrink is how much shorter the run gets because that pipe traveled on a diagonal. Steeper angle, marks closer together, more shrink, harder pull, more of the 360° budget. You will count the budget in lesson 6. Today, pick the shallowest angle whose spacing fits in the space you have.

These multipliers are the field chart, rounded. The exact spacing for 30° is 2, so 30° does not round. The 10° row is the coarse one: the chart says ×6 and 1/16, and the geometry is a little tighter than that. Box offsets are short, and the pencil is fatter than the difference. Use the chart unless the bender card prints its own.

## The rule for the tape

Say this with your hand on the drawing.

The tape starts at the end that stands for the box. You measure to the near edge, the place the offset has to be finished. You add the shrink to that measurement. That sum is the far mark, the second bend. The first bend sits back toward the box by depth times the multiplier.

Shrink is added because you are bending toward a point you already measured. Forget it and the rise finishes short of the obstruction every time. Take-up does not enter this layout. Anyone who subtracts 5 in. on an offset is solving a stub.

## Demo

Board, then the pipe. 6 in. rise, near edge 60 in. from the end, 30°.

Spacing: 6 × 2 = 12.
Shrink: 6 × 1/4 = 1-1/2.
Far mark: 60 + 1-1/2 = 61-1/2 from the end.
Near mark: 61-1/2 − 12 = 49-1/2 from the end.

Mark both, all the way around. Label them A (near, 49-1/2) and B (far, 61-1/2) so the class can talk about them.

Bend A first. Arrow on A, foot down, pull until the pipe sits on the 30° line. Stop. A degree of spring-back matters more on a shallow bend than on a 90, because the second bend has to match it. Creep to the line, release, and sight the line again. Nudge once if it fell back.

Roll the pipe 180° around its long axis. Arrow on B. Before you pull, sight down the stick. The first bend and the shoe should lie in one plane. If the first bend points off to the side, roll it until it doesn't. Then pull B to the same 30° line.

Set the piece on the bench. The two legs should both sit flat, or both read the same on a torpedo level. The rise should be 6 in. The place the rise finishes should land on your 60 in. mark on the bench, not on the 61-1/2 you wrote on the pipe. That gap is the shrink, and it is supposed to be there.

Call the lane while you roll the pipe. The first bend sticks up and will sweep.

## Their turn

They work the math on the stick, in marker, before any pull. You initial the two marks, then they bend.

**Piece 1.** 4 in. rise, near edge 36 in. from the end, 30°.

Spacing 8. Shrink 1. Far mark 37. Near mark 29.

**Piece 2.** 3 in. rise, near edge 20 in. from the end, 45°. Do the fractions on the board with them before they mark.

Spacing: 3 × 1.4 = 4.2 in., which is a hair over 4-3/16. Mark 4-3/16.
Shrink: 3 × 3/8 = 1-1/8.
Far mark: 20 + 1-1/8 = 21-1/8.
Near mark: 21-1/8 − 4-3/16 = 16-15/16.

**Piece 3, paper.** A 10 in. rise has to fit in 14 in. of run. Which angle.

30° wants 20 in. between marks. It does not fit.
45° wants 14 in. It fits.
60° wants 12 in. It fits, and it spends 120° instead of 90°.

They take 45°. The rule is the shallowest angle that fits.

## If the bend is wrong

Rise short, legs parallel: shrink was left out, or it was subtracted. The marks were right distances apart and in the wrong place.

Rise right, legs not parallel: the two angles do not match. Sight the degree line on the second bend before you pull, not after.

Legs parallel and the pipe corkscrews: the 180° roll was a guess. That is a dog leg. Scrap it. Do not unbend it.

First bend hits the floor before the second bend reaches its angle: the pipe is rolled the wrong way. Stop, roll it so the first bend rises off the floor, then pull.

## Close

One student states the 30° line from memory: times 2 between the marks, quarter inch of shrink per inch of rise, shrink added to the measurement, far mark at that sum, near mark stepped back.
