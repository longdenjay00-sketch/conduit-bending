# Lesson 2 — The stub-up

Time: 70 minutes. Group: demo at the board, then every student bends at least two stubs.

Students leave able to mark a 90 from the stamped take-up, bend it on the arrow, and measure the finished stub to the back of the bend.

## Before they come in

Board from lesson 1 still up, with this shoe's take-up. Combination square and a tape at the demo. 1/2 in. sticks in the rack. 3/4 in. and 1 in. shoes on the side bench for the second demo, if you have the pipe. If you only have 1/2 in. pipe, teach the other sizes on the board and bend 1/2 in. only.

Confirm the stamp again. The answer key below uses 5 in., 6 in., and 8 in. Rewrite the key on the board if your stamp differs. Do that before students mark.

## Open

A stub-up is a 90 with a length on it. The length in this class is measured to the back of the bend, the face that would sit against a wall or the side of a box. Say that before anyone touches a tape. Center-of-bend measurement is a different job and not this lab.

The bend is an arc. The arc uses up some of the height you wanted. The shoe's take-up is how much. You subtract it, mark that distance from the end, and set the arrow on the mark.

Write the line and leave it:

**mark = stub height − take-up**

Work one on the board with the stamp you wrote yesterday. For a shoe stamped 5 in., a 12 in. stub in 1/2 in. EMT is 12 − 5 = 7. The mark goes 7 in. from the end. Arrow on the mark. After the bend, the back of the 90 is 12 in. from the end.

Ask what a 4 in. stub does on that shoe. The mark would fall off the end of the pipe. This shoe cannot make a stub shorter than its take-up. The minimum stub is the number on the board.

## Demo

1/2 in. stick. Measure 7 in. from the end, square the mark all the way around. A mark on one side disappears once the pipe rolls.

Floor, hook, arrow on the mark, foot on the pedal. Pull past the 45 line without stopping to admire it, and ease off as the pipe comes up to the 90. Creep a degree past the line and hold. Release. Check with the square. If it came back to shy of 90, hook it again and nudge. If it went long, set the pipe in the shoe the other way and ease it back one push. One correction. A second correction starts a kink.

Measure from the end to the back of the bend. Call the number out. Within 1/8 in. of 12 is a pass.

Second demo, board only if you lack the pipe, on the floor if you have it. 3/4 in. EMT, shoe stamped 6, stub 15 in. Mark at 9 in. 1 in. EMT, shoe stamped 8, stub 14 in. Mark at 6 in. Same motion. The larger shoe takes more of your weight and less of your arms.

## Their turn

Each student marks and bends these, in order, on 1/2 in. unless you assigned them a larger shoe. They show you the mark before they pull.

| Piece | Pipe | Stub, to the back | Mark from the end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1/2 in. | 14 in. | 9 in. |
| 2 | 1/2 in. | 10 in. | 5 in. |
| 3 | 3/4 in., or board if you have no 3/4 | 16 in. | 10 in. |
| 4 | 1 in., or board | 20 in. | 12 in. |
| 5 | 1/2 in. | 4 in. | No mark. The take-up is 5 in. |

Piece 2 puts the mark at the take-up, 5 in. from the end. That is a legal layout and a short stub. Warn them the handle comes down close to the floor and the short end can clip the shoe. Foot stays planted.

Walk the marks. The usual miss is measuring to the near side of the marker line instead of squaring through the 7 in. point. The usual bend miss is a stub that grows because the arrow sat past the mark, toward the long leg.

## If the number is wrong

Measure before you let them "fix" it with another pull.

A stub taller than the target means the mark was too far from the end. They subtracted too little, or they set the arrow on the wrong side of the mark.

A stub shorter than the target means the mark was too close to the end. They subtracted too much. Using the 3/4 in. take-up on a 1/2 in. shoe does exactly this: every stub comes out an inch short.

A bend that is round and the right height, and shy of square, gets one nudge. A bend that is flat, wrinkled, or out of plane goes in the short bin.

## Close

Have one student run the 18 in. stub in 1/2 in. pipe out loud: 18 − 5 = 13, arrow on 13, back of the bend reads 18.

Tell them the next number they learn is not another take-up. An offset uses a multiplier and a shrink, and those get added and stepped off. Take-up stays here with the 90.
