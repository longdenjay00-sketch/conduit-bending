# Lesson 1 — The bender

Time: 45 minutes. Group: the whole class, one demo shoe, then pairs.

Students leave knowing the four marks on the shoe, which way the handle swings, and why a kinked stick goes in the scrap bin.

## Before they come in

Sweep a lane long enough for a 10 ft stick plus the handle swing. Set one 1/2 in. bender on the floor, the card beside it, glasses at the door. Clamp nothing yet. Put the adopted codebook on the bench, closed. You will open it in lesson 6. Today you only show them where it sits.

Read the stamp on the demo shoe. Write "1/2 in. take-up: ___ in." on the board with the stamped number. If the stamp says 5, write 5. The later scripts assume 5, 6, and 8. A different stamp means you change the answer keys, not the shoe.

## Open

Hand out glasses before anyone picks up a stick.

This class bends scrap EMT with a hand bender. The pipe on the rack is practice pipe. Installed conduit stays on the wall. A panel stays closed. If a bend you need in the building is already on a live run, that is a different crew and a different procedure. In this room the pipe starts straight and leaves as a fitting you can hold in two hands.

EMT bends because the shoe supports the wall of the tube. Heat is the wrong tool. A torch burns the coating off and the tube collapses. The shoe is the tool.

## The shoe

Hold the bender up so the marks face the class. Point, don't lecture past the point.

The arrow is the start of the bend. Stubs and offsets use the arrow.

The star sits where the back of a finished 90 will be. Back-to-back bends use the star. You will not use it today.

The teardrop, some shoes call it a rim notch, sits at the center of the radius. The middle bend of a three-point saddle uses it.

The degree lines are 10, 22.5, 30, 45, and 60. You stop when the pipe lines up with the line. You do not guess the angle from the handle.

The hook keeps the pipe in the groove. The pedal is where your foot goes, and it stays there until the bend is done. A light foot lets the pipe flatten. A flat pipe has a smaller inside than the tube you started with, and the wire will tell you about it on the pull.

Show the stamp. Read it aloud. Tell them the number on the board is the number this room subtracts for a stub on this shoe. A chart in a book does not outvote the casting in your hand.

## The pull

One scrap stick. You bend it. They watch the lane.

Set the hook on the pipe about 2 ft from one end. Arrow anywhere for this one; the mark does not matter. Foot on the pedal, pipe on the floor, body over the shoe. Pull the handle down in one steady motion until the pipe meets the 30° line. Stop. Show them the line.

Call the lane before the second pull. The handle comes back up, then down again. Anyone standing in that arc gets the handle. Send them to the wall.

Pull this one the rest of the way to 90 and hold it. Let go. The pipe eases back a degree or two. That is spring-back. On a real stub you will creep a degree past the line, then check with a square. You do not yank past 90 and call it close.

Slide a square up to the bend. Name what you see: a 90, the back of the bend, the stub end, the long leg.

## What ruins a stick

Pass the bent scrap around. Then pass a kinked scrap if you have one from a previous class. If you do not, do not make one for the demonstration.

A dog leg is two bends that do not share a plane. The pipe corkscrews. You can sight it by looking down the stick the way you sight a board. A dog leg does not get unbent. Unbending work-hardens the seam and it splits. The stick goes in the short bin. A straight end longer than 24 in. can be cut off and used for a later stub.

The wrong shoe does the same damage. A 1/2 in. shoe on 3/4 in. pipe flattens it. Match the stamp to the pipe. Say the size out loud before the hook goes on.

Cut ends are sharp. Today you are bending full sticks, so most of you will not cut. When you do cut, the reamer comes out before a coupling does. That rule gets a section number in lesson 6. For now the rule is: a burr does not go near a hand or a conductor.

## Their turn

Pairs. Each pair makes one bend on a scrap end: hook on, foot on, stop at 30°, show you the degree line, then take it to 90° and stop. You walk the lane and watch feet and fingers.

You are checking three things. The foot is on the pedal. The hands are on the handle and the pipe, clear of the shoe. The pull is one motion. A student who jerks the handle walks the pipe out of the groove.

No measurements this round. A pretty 90 with no target is the point.

## Close

Leave the board up: the take-up you wrote, and four words — arrow, star, teardrop, degrees.

Next lab is a stub with a number on it. They will subtract the take-up from the height they want, put the arrow on that mark, and bend to 90. Ask one student to say that back before you dismiss them. If they mention shrink, stop and separate the words. Shrink belongs to offsets. Take-up belongs to stubs. Those get mixed later if you let them mix today.
