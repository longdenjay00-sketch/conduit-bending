# Lesson 4 — The three-point saddle

Time: 75 minutes. Group: one demo over a clamped obstacle, then one saddle each.

Students leave able to move the center mark for shrink, space the wings, and bend center-then-wings so the pipe returns to the line it left.

## Before they come in

Clamp a round obstacle about 2 in. high to the bench, 15 in. from a mark you call the box end. A scrap of 1-1/2 in. or 2 in. pipe is ideal. A piece of rigid the students are not allowed to bend works. Measure the height they actually have to clear and use that height if it is not 2 in. Rewrite the demo numbers on the board from the measured height. The script's 2 in. numbers are for a 2 in. clearance.

Leave the offset chart up. Circle the 22.5° row. They need to see that a three-point saddle is not a new chart. The wings are an offset angle you already know, and the center is twice that angle.

## Open

A three-point saddle carries the pipe over a narrow round obstacle and puts it back on the same line. The center bend is 45°. Each wing is 22.5°. The wings face the opposite way from the center, and they face the same way as each other.

Count it out loud: 22.5 + 45 + 22.5 = 90. One saddle spends a quarter of the whole bend budget between two boxes. That is why you do not sprinkle these between every all-thread on a ceiling.

The teardrop is the center of the radius. It is the mark you set on the center of this saddle. The wings use the arrow. Mixing those two is how a saddle lands beside the pipe it was meant to clear.

## The layout

Measure from the box end to the center of the obstacle. Measure the height you need to clear. Give the pipe a little air if the obstacle is a pipe you must not rub. If you decide the clearance is 2 in., the depth in the math is 2 in. Write the depth down. Do not keep it in your head.

Then, for the chart this class uses:

- Shrink moves the center mark ahead, farther from the box, by 3/16 in. for each inch of depth.
- Each wing sits 2-1/2 in. from that center mark for each inch of depth.

The bender card wins if it prints a different saddle spacing. Some cards say 2.6, the same multiplier as a 22.5° offset. The difference on a 2 in. saddle is about 1/4 in. across both wings. Teach one number for the day and use it on every stick.

## Demo

Depth 2 in. Center of the obstacle 15 in. from the end.

Shrink: 2 × 3/16 = 3/8.
Center mark: 15 + 3/8 = 15-3/8.
Wing spacing: 2 × 2-1/2 = 5.
Near wing: 15-3/8 − 5 = 10-3/8.
Far wing: 15-3/8 + 5 = 20-3/8.

Mark all three around the pipe. Label them.

Bend the center first. Teardrop on 15-3/8, pull to 45°. This bend is the hump.

Roll the pipe 180°. Arrow on the near wing, pull to 22.5°. Sight the plane before you pull. Arrow on the far wing, pull to 22.5°. Both wings in the same plane as the center.

Set the saddle on the obstacle. The crown should clear it, the pipe on both sides should be back on the bench, and the crown should sit over the obstacle rather than beside it. A saddle that clears and sits 2 in. off to the side is a layout that was right and a roll that was wrong.

## Their turn

One saddle each. Depth 4 in. Center of the obstacle 30 in. from the end. You can clamp a taller block for the check, or check with a tape.

Shrink: 4 × 3/16 = 3/4.
Center mark: 30-3/4.
Spacing: 4 × 2-1/2 = 10.
Wings: 20-3/4 and 40-3/4.

They write the three marks and the words "teardrop" and "arrow" next to the right ones. You look before they bend. A student who puts the arrow on the center is about to move the crown by the distance between those two marks on the shoe. Stop the pull.

Second check, paper, while pairs wait for a bender. A 1 in. saddle, center at 22 in.

Shrink 3/16. Center mark 22-3/16. Spacing 2-1/2. Wings at 19-11/16 and 24-11/16.

## If the saddle is wrong

Crown past the obstacle, wings equal: the shrink was added the right amount and then the teardrop was set on the wrong side of the mark, or shrink was added twice. Measure the center mark before you diagnose the bend.

Pipe back on the bench on one side and still up on the other: the wings are different angles. Match them to the 22.5 line.

Pipe back down and dogged: the roll between the wings was short of 180°. Scrap. Do not press it flat in the shoe.

Both wings bent the same direction as the center: the pipe climbs and never comes back. That stick cannot be talked into a saddle. Short bin.

## Close

Ask for the degree total before they leave. Ninety. Ask which mark is on the center. The teardrop. Ask which chart row the wings use. The 22.5° row.

Next lab uses the star for the first time, on a back-to-back 90, and then stacks two offsets into a four-point saddle for an obstacle with a flat top. A three-point saddle on a wide duct makes a peak and a poor pull. The four-point is the tool for that shape.
