# Lesson 5 — Back-to-back and the four-point saddle

Time: 90 minutes. Group: one back-to-back demo, one four-point on the board, one four-point bent per pair.

Students leave able to put the star on a measured back, and able to lay a flat-top saddle out as two offsets with the flat left alone.

## Before they come in

A clear lane for a U bend, because the second 90 swings a long leg. The 4 in. × 8 in. block on the bench, near face 24 in. from a taped "box" line. Offset chart still posted. Star circled on a spare shoe if you have one to hold up.

## Open, part one — the U

A back-to-back is two 90s that make a U. Both bends face the same way, so the legs are parallel. You use it between two walls, two strut lines, or the sides of a rack.

The first 90 is the stub you already know. The second is measured to the back of the bend, so the star replaces the arrow. The star is the gain, built into the shoe. You do not memorize a separate gain number and you do not subtract take-up again. Shops that cut conduit to a calculated length for a 90 are doing a different layout. If that lesson comes later, you will measure a finished bend from this lab and write that shoe's gain on the board from the pipe, not from a chart.

## Demo — back-to-back

Bend a first 90 on a 1/2 in. stick with any comfortable stub, arrow and take-up, the lesson 2 way. Check it square.

Hook the tape on the back of that 90. Mark 36 in. along the pipe. Square the mark.

Set the star on the mark. The hook faces away from the first bend so the second 90 stands parallel to the first, not back into it. Foot on the pedal. The long leg is going to sweep. Clear it. Pull to 90°, creep for spring-back, check both legs with the square.

Measure outside to outside, back to back. The target is 36 in., tolerance 1/8 in.

The miss to catch: a student sets the arrow on the 36 in. mark out of habit. The second back lands short by about the distance from the arrow to the star. Show that distance on the shoe once, with a finger, so they can see how far the habit costs them.

If the two legs open away from each other like an S, the pipe was rolled between bends. A U does not get a 180° roll. An S made of two 90s is a legal shape and a terrible offset: it spends 180° to do what a 30° offset does in 60°. Point at one if it happens, name it, and put it in the short bin unless you want it as a sample on the bench.

## Open, part two — the flat top

A three-point saddle peaks. A duct, a cable tray, or a wide block wants a flat top. That is four bends: one offset up, a straight run across, one offset down.

This class makes those offsets at 30°. All four bends are 30°. The budget cost is 120°. Say that before anyone marks, so a student who was about to put this in the middle of a run full of 90s hears the number while the pipe is still straight.

Layout, using the same tape rule as lesson 3.

The first offset finishes at the near face of the obstacle. Add that offset's shrink to the near-face measurement. That sum is mark B, the start of the flat. Mark A sits back toward the box by the spacing.

The flat, B to C, is the width of the obstacle, measured on the pipe. Shrink does not go into the flat. The top runs parallel to the original line, so an inch of pipe is an inch across the obstacle.

Mark D sits past C by the same spacing. The second offset brings the pipe back down. Its shrink shortens the tail. If the tail has to reach a measured box, add a second shrink to that box measurement the same way you added the first. Leave B, C, and the flat where they are.

Directions: A and D match each other. B and C match each other, opposite of A. Finish the first offset and sight it before you start the second. A second offset bent the same way as the first climbs again. That piece is a double offset. It is scrap for this lab.

## Demo — four-point, on the board and then on the pipe

Block 4 in. high, 8 in. wide, near face 24 in. from the end. 30°.

Spacing: 4 × 2 = 8.
Shrink of one offset: 4 × 1/4 = 1.
Mark B: 24 + 1 = 25.
Mark A: 25 − 8 = 17.
Mark C: 25 + 8 = 33.
Mark D: 33 + 8 = 41.

All four from the same end, all the way around, labeled.

Bend A on the arrow to 30°. Roll 180°. Bend B on the arrow to 30°. Set it on the bench. You should have a clean 4 in. offset and the rise should finish on the near face.

Then bring it back down. Bend C so the raised leg returns toward the original line, arrow, 30°. Roll 180°. Bend D to 30°. Sight before each pull.

On the bench: the flat clears the block, both tails sit on the original line, and the four bends are in one plane. The tail is about 2 in. shorter than the 41 in. mark, because each offset contributed about an inch of shrink. Show that with the tape so the shrink stops being a rumor.

## Their turn

Per pair, one four-point. Change one number so they cannot copy the demo marks.

Depth 3 in., width 10 in., near face 28 in. from the end, 30°.

Spacing: 6.
Shrink: 3/4.
B: 28-3/4.
A: 22-3/4.
C: 38-3/4.
D: 44-3/4.

You initial A, B, C, D and the words "flat = 10" written between B and C. Then they bend.

Back-to-back is demo-and-watch unless the rack is full. If a pair finishes the saddle clean and time is left, they bend a 24 in. back-to-back on a fresh stick: first 90 by take-up, star on a mark 24 in. from the back.

## If it is wrong

Flat too short or too long, offsets otherwise clean: the width got the shrink treatment. Measure B to C on the straight pipe next time, and write the width on that segment before anyone adds a quarter inch to it.

Tail parallel and one offset high: the second pair matched the first pair's direction. Name it a double offset. Scrap.

U that measures 36 in. to the arrow's idea of the bend and short to the back: the arrow was on a star job.

## Close

Two questions. Which mark measures a back-to-back? The star, from the back of the first 90. What is the flat on a four-point equal to? The width of the obstacle, with shrink kept out of it.

Next lab is a single bend, the kick, and a paper count of degrees. Bring the codebook mentality even though the book stays on your bench: every bend they made this week has a number of degrees, and the run fails when the sum passes 360 between pull points.
